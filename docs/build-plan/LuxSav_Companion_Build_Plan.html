01 · The idea

Every travel app ends at checkout.
LuxSav starts there.

Booking platforms compete at the moment of purchase — inventory, photos, price. After that the traveller is on their own with PDF vouchers, WhatsApp threads and a driver who may or may not be outside arrivals. The days that matter most, the ones actually spent travelling, belong to no one.

LuxSav Companion claims those days. The itinerary stops being a document and becomes a live object that knows the time, the weather, the party, the budget and the free hours — and acts on them.

04 · The signatures in detail
01

Living Itinerary

The day re-plans itself — and asks the guest one question.
The momentThe flight lands ninety minutes late. Before the guest has cleared immigration, Tanova has moved the transfer, pushed back check-in, shifted the afternoon drive to tomorrow and told everyone involved. The guest sees one card: “Your afternoon has been rearranged. Keep it?”
Why it's new

Itineraries today are documents. When reality diverges, a person rebuilds them by phone. Here the itinerary is a model of times, durations and dependencies, so it can be solved again in seconds.

What LuxSav already has

Tanova already assembles day-by-day plans from each experience's duration and part-of-day slot. Every one of the 248 experiences carries both.

What we build

The trip as a dependency chain (flight → transfer → check-in → activity); triggers from flight status, weather and a “running late” button; a re-planning engine; supplier notifications.

Honest limit

Suppliers have to accept changes. Version 1: Tanova proposes and a LuxSav concierge confirms within minutes. Automatic confirmation arrives supplier by supplier as live connections land — the booking record already has a field for them.

02

Gap Commerce

Free time becomes the storefront.
The moment“You're free from 16:30 tomorrow. The Upper Zambezi sundowner cruise runs two hours and is back before dinner — US$208 for all four of you, well inside your remaining budget. Add it?”
Why it's new

Upselling today is a banner or an email. This is an offer that fits a specific hole in a specific day, for a specific party, inside the money they have left to spend.

What LuxSav already has

Live search on luxsav.com that filters experiences by time window, party size and total budget, and returns party pricing. It was built and deployed this month.

What we build

Gap detection across each day; travel time between points; availability checks; one-tap add that updates the itinerary, the payment and the lodge in a single step.

Honest limit

Travel time needs a location for every experience and lodge — none are stored yet. Suggestions also need real availability, not just the catalogue, before they can be promised.

03

True Price

The real total for this party, before they commit.
The momentA family picks Nantwich Lodge for two adults and a nine-year-old. The price on screen is US$6,810 — accommodation, the child rate, the conservation levy and park fees. Nothing is added at checkout, and nothing is added at the lodge.
Why it's new

Southern African pricing is notoriously hard to compare: per person sharing, per room, per unit, whole-camp buy-outs, flat or percentage single supplements, levies charged nightly, per stay or capped. Most platforms show a “from” price and let the invoice sort out the rest.

What LuxSav already has

32 distinct pricing models catalogued from 43 properties' 2027 rate sheets, including capped levies, per-stay fees and age-banded child rates — the hard part of the research is done.

What we build

A pricing engine that picks the right model for the party and dates; levy and park-fee tables; a way to load each supplier's new rates every year.

Honest limit

Rate sheets change annually and need a named owner. Launch with the suppliers already catalogued, and only label a price “all in” where the engine covers that supplier's full model.

04

Safari Offline

Built for the places LuxSav actually sells.
The momentNo signal at Mana Pools. The guest still opens tomorrow's plan, shows the voucher QR at the gate, and asks Tanova “what time is my pickup?” — and gets the right answer.
Why it's new

Travel apps assume connectivity. Many of the camps and parks LuxSav sells have none, which is precisely when a guest most needs their documents and their plan.

What LuxSav already has

The whole trip — days, items, stays and totals — is already one structured object returned by a single API request, which is exactly the shape that can be stored on a device.

What we build

On-device storage for the trip, vouchers and documents; offline map tiles for the trip's area; an offline Tanova that answers from the downloaded trip; a queue that syncs changes when signal returns.

Honest limit

Offline Tanova answers about the trip, not about the wider world. Booking still needs signal — requests wait in the queue and every screen shows when its data was last updated.

05

Connected Camp

Traveller and lodge, looking at the same trip.
The momentThe guest taps “running 40 minutes late”. The lodge's portal updates, the kitchen holds lunch, the guide moves the drive — and the guest gets a message from the camp itself, not a call centre.
Why it's new

Travel apps talk to travellers. Operators find out about changes by email, if at all. Here both sides of the journey look at one live record.

What LuxSav already has

LuxSav and its operator portal already run on one shared platform, and the booking record already stores the operator, the sales channel and property-system references.

What we build

An operator view of arriving and in-house guests; live changes and guest notes (dietary needs, celebrations, mobility); two-way messaging tied to the booking.

Honest limit

The value depends on operators using it. Start with LuxSav's own experiences and the lodges already in the rate book, and measure before expanding.

06

Border-Smart Journeys

Multi-country trips without the anxiety.
The momentA Victoria Falls to Chobe day crosses a border and back. Three days before, Tanova checks the passport in the guest's wallet has enough validity and blank pages, explains which visa covers the crossing, and reminds them what to carry for fees.
Why it's new

Multi-country trips are where travel in Southern Africa is most complex and where guests are most anxious. Apps treat each country as a separate booking.

What LuxSav already has

Itineraries and products that already span borders in a single trip — including a three-day Victoria Falls and Chobe package in the live catalogue.

What we build

A per-crossing rules library; document checks against the Wallet; reminders timed to the itinerary rather than the booking date.

Honest limit

Border rules change. Every rule needs an owner and a review date, a concierge fallback, and wording that is clearly guidance rather than legal advice.

Tanova, everywhere
The concierge behind all six works in the app and on WhatsApp, with the same trip context in both, and hands over to a human LuxSav expert without the guest repeating themselves. Limit: WhatsApp Business requires approval and carries per-conversation costs, so it launches after the in-app version is proven.

What the UI kit is — and isn't
It supplies 24 polished screens on Android and iOS — roughly the Explore, detail, booking, profile and onboarding half of the app. Like any template it runs on sample content, with no accounts and no connection to luxsav.com. Its value is time: it covers the familiar screens so the effort goes into the six signatures, which no kit contains.
Adapting it
Its colours live in one theme file, so the luxsav.com green-and-gold brand replaces the kit's teal accent and its colour picker quickly — though the kit uses its main colour in 79 places, for prices and icons as well as buttons, so each use is reviewed. The real work is its hotel-shaped data model: 29 files assume a “hotel with rooms per night”, while LuxSav sells stays, experiences, day trips and packages. That model is generalised once, early, rather than patched screen by screen.

Architecture decisions already taken in this plan
  • The app stays on the kit's existing GetX structure rather than mixing in a second state-management approach.
  • Every signature is computed on the platform, so the app, WhatsApp and the operator portal always agree.
  • Offline is designed in from the start — retrofitting it into a connected-only app is far more expensive.
  • Screens open through named routes, so a link from luxsav.com or a notification can open a specific trip or experience.
  • The app follows the luxsav.com brand exactly — colours, fonts and button styles come from the website's own stylesheet.

10 · Risks and decisions

What could go wrong, and what we need from you

RiskHow the plan handles it
Suppliers won't accept automated changesEvery change routes through a LuxSav concierge in version 1; automation is switched on supplier by supplier.
Rate data goes out of dateA named owner per supplier; the engine refuses to show “all in” on expired rates.
Operators don't use their sideStart with LuxSav's own experiences; measure before expanding to partner lodges.
Offline answers are wrongOffline Tanova answers only from the downloaded trip and always shows when it was last updated.
Border rules changeDated rules with a reviewer, a concierge fallback, and guidance wording — never presented as legal advice.
Six signatures at once is too muchThey ship one milestone at a time, and each must pass its “in your hand” test before the next begins.
Decisions needed before M1
  • Account model. Email and password, phone code, or both? A phone code suits WhatsApp-first travellers.
  • Languages at launch. English only, or English and Arabic for the Dubai market? The kit also ships French and Japanese.
  • Dark mode. luxsav.com has none — design one, or launch light-only?
  • Pilot destination for locations and Gap Commerce. Victoria Falls is recommended: 51 live experiences and the only top destination where every signature applies. Dubai has more inventory (66) but little use for most signatures.
  • In-app payment provider to sit alongside PayPal.
  • Owners for the rate book, border rules and destination content.
  • iOS timing — at launch in M5, or earlier for investor and partner demos.
  • Licence. Confirm the CodeCanyon licence covers this app.
